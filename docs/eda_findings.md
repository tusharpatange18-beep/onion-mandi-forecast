# EDA findings (Phase 2)

All numbers come from `docs/eda_numbers.md` (generated by `python -m mandi.run_eda`); figures are in `results/figures/`.
Five markets: Solapur, Pune, Vashi, Kolhapur, Sambhajinagar. Prices are modal price in Rs/quintal.

**Evidence rule.** Every finding that drives a modelling choice (seasonal period, transformation, differencing, features)
is computed on **2014-2019 only** and tagged **[TRAIN]**. Findings tagged **[FULL]** use 2014-2024 and are descriptive:
they were not used to choose anything. The runner asserts that its modelling slice ends before 2020-01-01.

## 0. How gaps were handled for ACF/PACF and ADF/KPSS

**Choice: weekly mean of the observed days** (week ending Sunday), a week counted only if it has at least 3 observed days,
and a gap of at most 1 week carried forward. No daily value is filled.

Why not the daily series forward-filled up to 3 days:
1. Every market closes on one fixed weekday (section 1). Forward-filling would put a fake "no change" on roughly 1 day in 7,
   which inflates the lag-1 autocorrelation and plants a false lag-7 pattern.
2. A quarter to a third of days are missing (23-33% of each market's span), in runs of up to 71 days, so a 3-day fill still leaves broken stretches.
3. The weekly mean averages real quotes only and removes the day-to-day quote noise (section 5), so the tests look at the price trend.

Cost: the tests describe weekly dynamics, not daily ones. For daily dynamics I report one extra table that uses only pairs of
days that were both actually observed (no filled values). Coverage [TRAIN]: valid weeks 262 / 239 / 286 / 274 / 187
(Kolhapur / Pune / Sambhajinagar / Solapur / Vashi); the 1-week fill adds only 3-11 weeks per market; the longest unbroken
stretch is 242 / 243 / 274 / 266 / 190 weeks. ADF/KPSS use that longest stretch; the same tests on all valid weeks joined
end to end give the same readings.

## 1. Missing days are mostly a fixed weekly closure, plus holidays [TRAIN, confirmed in 2020-24]

| Market | Closed on | Share of missing days that are this closure | Unexpected missing, share of open days |
|---|---|---|---|
| Solapur | Sunday | 44% | 21.1% |
| Pune | Saturday | 68% | 8.0% |
| Vashi | Sunday | 60% | 11.3% |
| Kolhapur | Sunday | 52% | 15.3% |
| Sambhajinagar | Friday | 50% | 16.5% |

- On the closure weekday, 99-100% of days are missing. In 2014-2024 there are only 0-6 quotes on a closure weekday per market.
  The closures hold in 2020-2024 as well. This is why the earlier "Sundays are the gaps" assumption in the brief was wrong.
- "Unexpected" missing days (not the closure weekday) are 8-21% of open days. They cluster on **public holidays**: on 26 Jan, 1 May and
  15 Aug, 82-84% of the open markets are missing, against 15% on other days (2 Oct: 42%). Days flagged as festivals in the file:
  51.6% unexpected-missing vs 13.1% on other open days. (The holiday dates are calendar knowledge, not looked up.)
- Missingness is worse early: 2014 unexpected-missing is 39% (Kolhapur, from its first quote in June), 47% (Sambhajinagar) and 50% (Solapur) [FULL].
- Markets miss the same days: both-missing happens 2-7 times more often than independence would give (phi 0.17-0.51;
  highest Kolhapur-Vashi 0.51 and Solapur-Vashi 0.46) [TRAIN]. 158 days in 2014-2024 have 4 or more of the 5 markets missing [FULL].
- Is missingness related to price or arrivals? [TRAIN] Not in a consistent way. By tercile of the local price level the
  missing rate differs significantly in three markets but not monotonically (Kolhapur 7.6 / 14.0 / 11.2%, Pune 9.2 / 4.7 / 9.8%,
  Sambhajinagar 11.2 / 16.7 / 9.2%; Solapur and Vashi no difference). The one clear link is **Sambhajinagar arrivals**: the lowest
  arrivals tercile has 19.7% missing vs 7.7% and 9.8% (p < 0.001). The data is therefore not missing completely at random.
- COVID spring 2020 [FULL, descriptive]: 31 days between 23 Mar and 30 Jun 2020 have 4+ markets missing; the longest gaps are
  Pune 8 Apr-30 May (53 days), Solapur 4 Apr-7 Jun (65), Vashi 8 Apr-3 May (26), Kolhapur 17 Apr-5 May (19). A second cluster
  falls on 16-27 Oct 2020; I cannot say why from this data.

**Modelling consequence.** Closure days and holidays are known from the calendar, so they are not surprises. Evaluate only on observed days
(already the rule). Lag features must be "last observed quote on or before t-k", not "the value exactly k days ago", because the exact day is
missing about a quarter of the time or more.

## 2. Log price is close to a random walk [TRAIN]

| Market | ADF p, level | KPSS p, level | ADF p, 1st difference | AR(1) coefficient (weekly) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Kolhapur | 0.53 | >= 0.10 | 0.00 | 0.987 |
| Pune | 0.008 | >= 0.10 | 0.006 | 0.985 |
| Sambhajinagar | 0.24 | >= 0.10 | 0.00 | 0.983 |
| Solapur | 0.63 | >= 0.10 | 0.00 | 0.984 |
| Vashi | 0.80 | 0.025 | 0.00 | 0.999 |

- Levels: ADF cannot reject a unit root in four of five markets (Pune rejects). KPSS (p clipped at 0.10 by statsmodels) does not reject
  stationarity, except in Vashi where both tests say non-stationary. The two tests disagree in three markets (Kolhapur, Sambhajinagar, Solapur), which with 190-274 weeks
  usually means low power; it is not proof of stationarity.
- Differences are stationary in every market (ADF p <= 0.006, KPSS p >= 0.10). Weekly AR(1) coefficients of 0.98-0.999 say the
  best guess for next week is very close to this week.
- **Consequence: the naive "last observed price" baseline will be hard to beat**, especially at 7 days. Work with log differences or with
  the change relative to the last observed price; one level-differencing (d = 1) is supported.

## 3. Use log price [TRAIN]

- Skewness of price 1.9-2.7 falls to 0.55-1.03 for log price.
- Rolling standard deviation of daily price changes against rolling mean price (60-day windows) has a log-log slope of
  0.95-1.14 (Kolhapur 1.12, Pune 0.98, Sambhajinagar 1.14, Solapur 1.00, Vashi 0.95): variability grows in proportion to the level.
- **Consequence: model log price; report errors on the Rs scale too.**

## 4. Seasonality

- **Weekday, price [TRAIN]: none worth modelling.** The average weekday effect on price is within +-1.8% and only 3 of 30 intervals exclude zero
  (about 1.5 would be expected by chance). The weekday effect on **arrivals** is large (up to +50% on Sambhajinagar Sundays,
  +36% Thursdays; +27% Vashi Mondays; +16-18% Kolhapur Mondays, Pune Sundays, Solapur Saturdays) but it does not carry into price.
  Consequence: a weekly seasonal period of 7 adds little for price itself.
- **Yearly [TRAIN]: a recurring but irregular pattern.** Counting market-years (22 in all), the highest month falls in Nov-Jan in 19 of 22
  (the three exceptions are all 2015, which peaked in August) and the lowest month in Feb-May in 17 of 22. The size and exact month vary: the average
  year profile spans 132-258% from low to high, but the year-to-year correlation of the monthly profiles is only 0.06-0.25 and
  STL seasonal strength is 0.04 (Sambhajinagar), 0.19 (Kolhapur), 0.29 (Vashi), 0.37 (Solapur), 0.44 (Pune). Only 5-6 yearly cycles exist,
  so the amplitude is poorly determined.
  Consequence: month-of-year or day-of-year features are worth testing, but "price 365 days ago" will be a weak seasonal-naive benchmark
  because the price level changes a lot between years (yearly medians range from 475 to 1,400 across the five markets in 2016-2019 [TRAIN]).

## 5. Daily noise versus weekly persistence [TRAIN]

- Daily log changes, using only pairs of observed days: lag-1 autocorrelation is negative in all markets
  (Kolhapur -0.15, Pune -0.36, Sambhajinagar -0.32, Solapur -0.07, Vashi -0.26). Beyond lag 1 it is mostly within +-0.18.
  A daily quote has a noise part that reverses the next day.
- Weekly log changes: small positive autocorrelation at lags 1-2 (lag 1: 0.08-0.17; lag 2: 0.07-0.22). Only Pune's lags 1-2 clearly
  exceed the approximate 95% band (about 0.13); the others are inside it or borderline. At lag 26 the values are -0.03 to -0.12.
  PACF at lags 1-2 is 0.06-0.19 and at lags 3-4 at most 0.10, which suggests at most a low-order AR on the weekly differences.
- **Consequence:** a single day's quote contains noise that reverses the next day. This suggests (not proves) that a forecast anchored on a
  recent mean of observed days could beat one anchored on the last day, so a smoothed-naive baseline is worth testing next to the plain naive one.

## 6. The markets move together [TRAIN]

- Correlation of weekly log price changes 0.57-0.72 (Kolhapur-Pune 0.72 highest, Pune-Sambhajinagar 0.57 lowest); of log levels 0.92-0.98.
- Cross-correlations at +-1 and +-2 weeks are 0.02-0.31 on both sides, so no market clearly leads: Pune leads Sambhajinagar by one week at 0.31,
  Solapur leads Sambhajinagar by one week at 0.30, but most pairs are weak and symmetric.
- **Consequence:** other markets' past prices are legitimate features (known at the forecast origin), but expect modest gains over a
  market's own history because most of the common movement happens within the same week. One could also pool the five series.

## 7. Arrivals are a weak signal [TRAIN]

- Weekly log price vs log arrivals: correlation -0.60 (Kolhapur), -0.31 (Sambhajinagar), -0.30 (Pune), -0.21 (Solapur), -0.07 (Vashi).
  Weekly changes in the same week: |r| <= 0.15.
- Arrivals change k weeks earlier vs price change: noise band is about +-0.12-0.14. Only Kolhapur (-0.23) and Pune (-0.20) at k = 1 stand out.
- Same-day arrivals cannot be used at forecast time. **Consequence: use arrivals only lagged, and expect little gain**; this is the ablation to report.

## 8. Breaks and events

- **No formal instability in 2014-2019 [TRAIN].** CUSUM test of an AR(1) on weekly log price: p = 0.14 / 0.15 / 0.24 / 0.27 / 0.10
  (Kolhapur / Pune / Sambhajinagar / Solapur / Vashi), none significant at 5%. The test has low power for a few large jumps, so this is not
  proof of stability. Volatility of weekly changes is 5-19% higher in 2018-2019 than 2014-2017.
- **Large price swings coincide with policy dates [FULL, descriptive only, no causal claim].** Median price 4 weeks after vs 4 weeks before:
  Aug 2015 (E2) +75% to +120%; Dec 2015 (E3) -23% to -61%; 14 Sep 2020 ban (E9) +59% to +125%; 28 Oct 2023 MEP (E11) +58% to +98%;
  8 Dec 2023 ban (E12) -43% to -55%. The 29 Sep 2019 ban (E5) shows no jump (+0% to -20%); prices were already high. Prices were already
  moving before most of these dates, so these windows do not show an effect of the policy.
- **The price level in 2020-2024 is generally higher than in 2014-2019 [FULL].** Yearly medians in 2020-2024 range from 750 to 2,550
  against 475-1,400 in 2016-2019. This did not drive any choice: the case for modelling changes rather than raw levels rests on section 2
  (2014-2019 only), and this is only a description of what the test period looks like.
- A model trained on 2014-2019 sees one export ban (E5, September 2019) plus several MEP changes (E1-E4); the COVID period is entirely out of sample.

## 9. What I could not conclude

- The cause of any missing stretch (strikes, holidays, reporting lapses) is not in the data. I only show when and where it happens.
- With 5-6 yearly cycles and few events in 2014-2019, seasonality and event effects are weakly identified. The yearly seasonality
  should be treated as something the models are allowed to use, not as something the data proves.
- The weekly view hides daily dynamics. The daily table uses observed pairs only and covers lags up to 14 days (figure 06c).
- ADF/KPSS were run on 190-274 weeks per market, so their power is limited; the two tests disagree on levels in three markets.

## 10. Proposals for Phase 3 (not decisions; they need your confirmation)

1. Model **log price**; present errors in Rs as well.
2. Baselines: plain naive (last observed price), a smoothed naive (mean of the last few observed days), and **seasonal-naive**.
   Weekday price effects are within 1.4%, so seasonal-naive with period 7 is essentially "the price one week ago", a strong benchmark.
   A 365-day version is weak. Which one is the headline benchmark? That choice changes the headline improvement figure, so you should decide.
3. Differencing: one level difference (d = 1) in SARIMA; no seasonal difference supported by the evidence.
4. Features, all built on a calendar-day grid from the last observed quote at or before t-k: lags, rolling mean and std over observed days,
   month-of-year or Fourier terms, public-holiday and festival flags, days since last observed quote, other markets' lagged prices,
   arrivals only lagged. Weather stays in the later ablation.
5. Evaluate only on target dates with an actual quote; report the share of missing target days per market and horizon, and metrics with and
   without stale dates.
